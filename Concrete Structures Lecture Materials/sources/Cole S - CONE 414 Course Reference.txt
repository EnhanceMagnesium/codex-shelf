CLASSIFY EACH PROBLEM BEFORE CALCULATING

Read every subpart and identify whether it asks for section selection, effective flange width, applied-load demand, required reinforcement, nominal capacity, design capacity, or another result. Use only the calculation chain needed for that request.

For section selection, use the class span and support-condition rules, state assumptions for missing geometry, choose an allowed practical section, and distinguish a code/table minimum from the selected trial dimensions.

For reinforcement design, transcribe the loads and section first. Add self-weight only when appropriate to the stated dead load. Apply the class load combinations and analysis method, label the moment regions and support faces, calculate required steel using the correct class table or method, and complete minimum/maximum steel, strain, strength, and spacing checks when required.

For capacity problems with a given section and reinforcement, calculate the requested nominal or design strength from those givens. Do not add load combinations, moment coefficients, or a demand check unless the assignment asks for them.

For T-sections, determine effective flange width from the actual slab and web geometry and show the applicable limits before selecting the controlling width. Distinguish a clear gap, a center-to-center spacing, a flange overhang, and the total effective width. Examine exterior and interior beams separately when their geometry differs. For flexural strength, test the trial compression-block depth against the flange thickness before choosing the flange-only or flange-plus-web calculation. Then calculate the neutral-axis depth, steel strain, and the class strength factor.

For continuous beams, establish support continuity and clear span before selecting class coefficients. Identify each support face explicitly. Use the lecture's moment sign convention and place reinforcement on the tension face. Do not transfer a coefficient from one face or span region to another without checking its class-table label.

For any problem type not covered by the current modules, identify the missing course rule and seek the relevant supplied module or appendix. Do not invent a class convention. State a necessary assumption briefly and distinguish it from a given fact.

Shear method from Module 6:
- Printed slides 3–4 (PDF pages 4–5) define V_n = V_c + V_s, require φV_n ≥ V_u, give φ = 0.75 for shear and torsion, V_c = 2b_wd√f'_c, and V_s = A_vf_yd/s. A_v is the total area of stirrup legs. The slide lists #3 area as 0.11 in² and #4 as 0.20 in²; verify bar areas against the class table when a problem uses them.
- Printed slide 5 (PDF page 6) sequence: draw the shear-force diagram to find V_u; calculate φV_c; choose stirrup size and A_v. If V_u ≤ φV_c/2, the slide says no stirrups are required. If φV_c/2 < V_u ≤ φV_c, select the smaller spacing from A_vf_y/(50b_w) and A_vf_y/(0.75b_w√f'_c). If V_u > φV_c, calculate V_s = V_u/φ − V_c, then s = A_vf_yd/V_s.
- Printed slide 6 (PDF page 7) adds maximum spacing: for φV_c/2 < V_u ≤ φV_c, use the smaller of d/2 and 24 in. For V_u > φV_c, use the smaller of d/2 and 24 in when V_s ≤ 4b_wd√f'_c; use the smaller of d/4 and 12 in when V_s > 4b_wd√f'_c.
- Printed slide 7 (PDF page 8) locates the class critical shear section at distance d from the support face. Read the assignment figure and load location before applying the near-support or beam-ledged diagrams.
- Printed slides 8–9 (PDF pages 9 and 13) are worked-example references only. Their spans, loads, strengths, and stirrup selections are not defaults for later problems. In particular, Example 1 says its service loads exclude beam self-weight; honor that statement only when working that example or when a new assignment says the same.

Torsion method from Module 6:
- Printed slide 10 (PDF page 16) introduces torsional demand and the roles of concrete, closed stirrups, and longitudinal reinforcement.
- Printed slide 11 (PDF page 17) gives the class threshold for neglecting torsion, a combined shear–torsion concrete-section adequacy check, and the geometry definitions A_cp = bh, p_cp = 2(h+b), x_1 = b − (2 cover + stirrup diameter), y_1 = h − (2 cover + stirrup diameter), A_oh = x_1y_1, A_o = 0.85A_oh, and p_h = 2(x_1+y_1). Clear cover is measured to the outside of the hoop; the centerline dimensions subtract one stirrup diameter in total, not two. Transcribe the exact printed equation for the applicable problem and check it against the assigned appendix; do not reconstruct a blurred symbol from OCR.
- Printed slide 12 (PDF page 30) gives T_n = 2A_oA_tf_yt cotθ/s = T_u/φ and A_t/s = T_u/(2φA_of_yt cotθ). The slide defines A_t as the area of one stirrup leg required for torsion only and uses φ = 0.75 for shear and torsion.
- Printed slide 13 (PDF page 31) is a torsion example. Treat its demand, material properties, cover, and #4 closed stirrups as example-specific.
